## Entrance Design

Entrance design is what makes or breaks a PvP castle. Given a fair raid in an official server, the best entrance design paired with good players makes the castle unbreachable. The opposite can be said wherein a bad entrance design gives anyone a chance to have an easy breach.

If you are confident in handling raid defense while breached, you are probably the best clan in the server and any or no entrance design will work.

The main purpose of an entrance design is to prevent your castle from being breached.

## Sunbase

It is a strong strategy but ultimately fails on the higher level of play since enemies can just use daytime to summon and wait for golems to cook. Doing a sunbase also means reducing the size of the castle which makes raiding a lot quicker.

If the plot is naturally a sunbase, go through with it. Otherwise, having more doors before Castle Heart is better than having a sunbase.

## Stairs or no stairs at the entrance

Use stairs (most of the time). The only time you should remove stairs is when you have an overwhelming damage (AoE spells) coming from the sides that can only be casted if there are no stairs.

Having stairs forces the golem to go inside the fighting pit. The side railings makes the stairs more slender hence making it more deadly. The downside is that the stairs can be hit by the golem to reset the Castle Under Attack timer.

Meanwhile, having no stairs lets the golem do a big damage on your outmost door without much commitment. It also keeps the naturally wide ramp allowing attackers to have more room to easily dodge attacks while on it. As a silver lining, it at least allows ground target spells to be casted on it like Void, Bone Explosion, Rain of Chaos, etc.

## Fighting pit

The fighting pit is usually a 2x2 or 2x3 area after the initial stairs that is accessible to everyone. The stairs acts as a funnel to anyone who dares to go in or out of the castle. The pit is usually where the golem stays to deal some structural damage hence why this is the most important part of your entrance design. It is the most contested position in a raid defense. Having the best pit makes raid defense a lot easier.

The best fighting pit:

- has one narrow entrance and **exit**
- enables servants to detect attackers more quickly
- allows the defenders to position themselves as best as possible and as fast as possible
- locks attackers easily
- allows the defenders to evaluate the situation at a glance
- is surrounded by deep rooms that lets the defender do continuous damage with very minimal risk, even as much as allowing them to cast all their skills at max range

## 2x2 fighting pit

Classic. Stairs followed by 2x2 room surrounded by 3 wide doors and 1 small door or a wide stairs followed by 2x2 room surrounded by 3 wide doors. Seamless path connecting all the wide doors to allow defenders to move around and position themselves as best as possible when fighting at the pit.

Each big door can be camped by a twinbladeE+reaperQ user to kidnap anyone who gets close enough and lock them in for a very favorable fight outside the castle.

I do not advise using stairs + 2 wide doors to block the siege golem's range attack from the stairs. No one really does that and golem's range attack is so easy to handle.

## 2x3 fighting pit

A symmetric design usually used in plots where 2 stairs is used to create a chokepoint. The bigger gap between the 2 side doors can be an issue for the servant's aggro range. The bigger gap also gives more space for the attackers to play around which lessens the effectiveness of twinbladeE+reaperQ combo.

Again, I do not advise using stairs + 2 wide doors to block the siege golem's range attack.

## Freeform

This is for unique cases where a small fighting pit is not ideal. Often used in plots with an open area or plots where multiple entrances are being merged. Some principles I try to adhere:

- All golem positions should be covered by multiple safe defensive positions.
- Servants should be placed in a location where they can reliably draw aggro.

## Open Hole Trap

Intentionally leaving an open hole to invite attackers to drop in only for them to be locked. You can also use the holes to run away and prevent them from chasing. On this trap, the only way out is to breach the castle and break the doors.

## Corner Trap

Commonly done using L stairs on plots with 2 levels. By putting L stairs on the ground level beside the stairs going to 2nd level, you can have a deadly corner that is hard to get out of. However, trapped players can get out using Claw E, Arctic Leap, Mist Trance, Wolf Jump, Horse Jump, and Frog Jump.

## Final Checks

Always make sure that there are no ways to bypass your entrances. Look out for frog jumps, neighboring castles, glitches, and rock formations beside your castle. If you're not confident, it's best to ask someone that had meaningful experience on your chosen plot.

Your entrance design would be useless if enemy can just frog jump all the way to the Castle Heart.
